Measured performance

180 pages.
About four minutes.

Our recorded Gemma pipeline benchmark processed 30 documents containing 180 pages in 239.6 seconds. That is roughly 2,700 pages per hour across the run.

A separate run processed 63 pages in 80.2 seconds, or about 2,800 pages per hour. These are processing measurements, not a guaranteed rate for every deployment.

What that means for your firm

Use page count to size capacity: 50 short documents and 50 large scanned returns are very different workloads.

The measured pipeline includes document rendering, detection, redacted PDF output, and automated evaluation checks. Initial uploads, model startup, downloads, and human review are outside the timer.

Scan quality, page complexity, concurrent work, and your GPU change throughput. A sample batch on your server is the best way to establish your firm's expected speed.

Reference notes

Configuration and pricing checked September 5, 2026. The live worker configuration uses Gemma 4 E2B, with g6.xlarge preferred and g5.xlarge as a capacity fallback. AWS Price List API returned $0.8048/hour for shared-tenancy Linux g6.xlarge On-Demand in US East (N. Virginia). Monthly examples multiply that rate by 176 and 730 hours; they do not include discounts or other services.

See AWS On-Demand pricing, G6 specifications, and G5 specifications for current details.

Benchmark basis: saved Gemma evaluation summaries from August 29, 2026: 180 pages / 239.613 seconds and 63 pages / 80.201 seconds of elapsed wall time. Hourly rates are calculated as pages × 3,600 / elapsed seconds. The summaries do not record the exact GPU, so these measurements are a pipeline reference, not a certified g6.xlarge benchmark. Only aggregate statistics are published; client documents and individual results remain private.
